Building a DIY RC Car: Transmitter & Receiver from Scratch

May 04, 2026 · 12 min read · By ElectricTechs Team
RC cars are the perfect playground for learning electronics. In this build we design our own 2.4 GHz transmitter and receiver pair, wire it to a brushed motor controller, and dial in the throttle and steering channels. No proprietary remotes required.

Designing the Transmitter

We're using an Arduino Nano paired with an NRF24L01 radio module. Two thumb joysticks feed the analog inputs — one for steering, one for throttle. The whole thing fits on a small protoboard inside a 3D-printed grip.

Building the Receiver

On the car side, a second NRF24L01 listens for packets and forwards them to a small microcontroller, which generates PWM for the ESC and servo. Keep the radio at least 2 cm away from the motor wires to avoid noise.

Wiring the Motor & Servo

Power the logic side from a clean 5V BEC. Power the motor from the main LiPo through the ESC. Common ground is mandatory — half of all 'mystery glitches' in DIY RC cars are missing ground links between battery, ESC and receiver.

Tuning the Channels

Map joystick centers to PWM 1500 µs, full deflection to 1000 / 2000 µs, then add a small dead-zone in firmware so the car doesn't creep when the sticks are at rest.

⚠️ Safety first: Electrical work can be dangerous. Always turn off power at the breaker, verify with a tester, and follow local codes. When in doubt, call a licensed electrician.
